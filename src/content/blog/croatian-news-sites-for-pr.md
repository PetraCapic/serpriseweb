Our catalogue holds 335 news sites from Croatia and the wider region. 186 of them label a paid article as sponsored, 92 publish it without a label, and for 57 we have not checked yet. We pulled DR and traffic for every site from Ahrefs on 28 October 2026.

When we asked ChatGPT "Koji su najbolji hrvatski portali za objavu PR članaka" ("Which are the best Croatian news sites for publishing PR articles"), it cited [Index.hr's price list](https://cjenik.index.hr/), two foreign guest post aggregators and a couple of lists. None of the sources compared the sites on numbers. So we wrote this article.

## Goal first, then the site

A PR article on a news site is bought for one of three things. A site that is good for one is often bad for another.

| Goal | What to look at | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A link for Google | DR, topical relevance, the link's rel attribute | ebit.hr (DR 51) for tech and web |
| Traffic and readers | Organic traffic, category, audience | direktno.hr, 219,454 organic visits a month |
| AI visibility | Whether ChatGPT cites that site for your queries | regional sites for local services |

An article on a big news site brings reach and your name in the media. For the link alone, the value for money is usually weaker than on a smaller topical site.

## Business and tech sites: watch out for DR without traffic

For B2B services (agencies, software, consulting), business news sites matter most.

| Site | DR | Organic traffic/mo |
| --- | --- | --- |
| poslovni.hr | 73 | 82,795 |
| lidermedia.hr | 68 | 46,281 |
| bug.hr | 67 | 66,723 |
| ictbusiness.info | 55 | 431 |
| seebiz.eu | 52 | 8,610 |
| ebit.hr | 51 | 1,098 |
| ecommerce.hr | 50 | 555 |

Look at the last three rows. ictbusiness.info has DR 55 and 431 organic visits a month, ecommerce.hr DR 50 and 555, ebit.hr DR 51 and 1,098. For comparison, seebiz.eu has a slightly lower DR and eight times more traffic.

DR measures how many other sites link to a site, not how many people read it. High DR and low traffic mean that a link from that site is worth something to Google, but it will not bring you readers. A site like that makes sense if you need a link, and none if you want someone to read your article.

## A mention is worth more than a link

Ahrefs looked at [75,000 brands](https://ahrefs.com/blog/ai-overview-brand-correlation/) to see what correlates most with brand visibility in Google's AI Overviews. Brand mentions on the web had a correlation of 0.664, and the number of backlinks 0.218. Ahrefs itself notes that correlation does not prove causation.

For choosing a news site, this means the following: the goal "get mentioned on a site that AI reads" is probably worth more than the goal "get a high-DR link". The company name, city and service in the text work even when the link has no SEO value at all.

## The label is not the problem, a bad article is

<div data-embed="portali-oznake"></div>

A paid article should be labelled. Its value does not come from looking like news, but from the topic, the site's audience and whether AI cites that site. A good labelled article on the right site is worth more than an unlabelled piece nobody reads.

The fact that 92 sites publish without a label is a fact about the market, not a recommendation. We do not recommend unlabelled paid articles.

<aside class="pw-legal">

### What the law and Google say

- **EU:** [Directive 2005/29/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/HR/TXT/?uri=CELEX:32005L0029) on unfair commercial practices, Annex I, point 11, lists using editorial content in the media to promote a product where a trader has paid for the promotion, without making that clear, among the practices that are considered unfair in all circumstances.
- **Croatia:** the provision was transposed into the [Consumer Protection Act](https://narodne-novine.nn.hr/clanci/sluzbeni/2022_02_19_203.html) (Zakon o zaštiti potrošača, NN 19/22), Article 37, point 11. It treats as a misleading commercial practice to "koristiti uredničke sadržaje u medijima radi plaćene promidžbe proizvoda, a da u tim sadržajima nije jasno izraženo riječima, znakovima ili zvukovima koje potrošač može jasno prepoznati, da je riječ o promidžbi" (use editorial content in the media for paid promotion of a product without making it clear, in words, images or sounds the consumer can clearly recognise, that it is a promotion).
- **Google:** its [spam policies](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/essentials/spam-policies) list buying and selling links for ranking as link spam. A paid link is not a violation if it has [`rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"`](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/qualify-outbound-links), and Google recommends sponsored.

This is not legal advice. For a specific case, talk to a lawyer.

</aside>

So tell the two kinds of publication apart:

1) **Free publication because the story is worth it.** An editor publishes the piece because there is something new in it: research, a number, the first case of its kind in Croatia. There is no payment, so no label either. Business sites are happy to take stories like these.
2) **Paid publication.** Always labelled as sponsored content, with a link that has `rel="sponsored"` or `rel="nofollow"`.

## What a publication costs

News sites rarely publish their prices. We calculated the ranges below from quotes for a basic publication that we collected in October 2026 for 334 sites in the catalogue. "Most prices" is the middle 50% of prices.

| Category | Sites | Most prices | Median | Highest |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Big national (news, DR 70+) | 16 | €310 to €600 | €420 | €1,134 |
| Business and tech | 37 | €100 to €270 | €140 | €756 |
| Regional | 103 | €50 to €130 | €90 | €392 |
| Lifestyle | 53 | €100 to €420 | €200 | €1,134 |

The price for the same site depends heavily on format and section. On its [public price list](https://cjenik.index.hr/), Index.hr asks from €500 (Plus section) to €1,700 (Vijesti, its news section) for a PR article, with 3 hours on the homepage, and from €1,600 to €3,700 for a native article. So treat the ranges in the table as a frame, and ask for the exact price for the format you need.

## What we saw in ChatGPT answers

In two tests, [45 answers about agencies](/en/blog/chatgpt-citations-croatia/) and [12 answers about obituaries and funerals](/en/blog/ai-visibility-case-study-obituaries/), ChatGPT cited news sites like this:

- **For buying queries about agencies** it cited lists and price lists: assemblio.hr and index.hr 4 times each, tako.hr 3 times. Classic news articles (24sata.hr, slobodnadalmacija.hr, mojnovac.hr) were cited once each.
- **For local queries** it cited regional sites: novilist.hr and the Novi list classifieds for Rijeka and Zadar, rijekadanas.com, kaportal.net.hr, sibenik.in, likemetkovic.hr.

For a local service (a clinic, a lawyer, a restaurant, a repair shop), an article on the regional site of your city is therefore often worth more than one on a national site. The catalogue has 103 regional sites, from Istria to Slavonia.

## Before you pay for a publication

1) Check the organic traffic trend in Ahrefs for the last 12 months. A decline is a red flag.
2) Check whether Google indexes the sponsored articles section, e.g. `site:portal.hr/sponzorirano`. If it is not in Google, it is not in AI answers either.
3) Count the outbound links in a few paid articles. An article with lots of links to different companies reads like a noticeboard.
4) See how many paid articles the site publishes a day. If it is 20 or more, paid content is the site's main business.
5) Ask which rel attribute the link gets and check it in the code of the published article.
6) Ask whether the article stays up permanently or is removed after a while.
7) Ask whether the article gets a spot on the homepage or social media, and for how long.
8) Ask ChatGPT 5 questions your customers ask and note whether it cites that site.
9) Check how the site labels paid content. The label must be clearly visible to the reader.
10) Compare the site's category with your topic. A topically close audience is worth more than a big number.

## A first paragraph AI can easily cite

AI takes a sentence from an article, not the whole text. So the first sentence should stand on its own: a claim, a number, the company name and the city.

> [Company] from [city] [claim] [number] [when or compared with what].

Example: "The SERPRise agency from Žaborić near Šibenik analysed 45 ChatGPT answers and found that for buying queries, AI cites lists and price lists more often than agency blogs."

## How to choose, step by step

1) Put 5 questions your customers ask into ChatGPT (the list is in the article [The 20-question check](/en/blog/ai-visibility-check/)). Note which sites it cites.
2) If a site keeps coming up, it is your first choice, whatever its DR.
3) If no site is cited, pick a topical site with real traffic and go through the checklist above.
4) If your story is news (research, a number, a first case), offer it to an editor before you pay for a publication.
5) In the article, link to the page that answers the customer's question (a service, a price list), not the homepage.
